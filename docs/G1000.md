## The short version

**Two builds already exist.**

- **Build #1** — original G1000, **hand-wired**. Arduino UNO on a Keyestudio
  proto shield, several hundred point-to-point wires, orange 3D-printed
  bracketry. No PCB at all.
- **Build #2** — G1000 **NXi**, PCB-based. FlightSimMaker shield driving the
  GMA1347 audio panel over two ribbon cables. Working.

**The shield is a THIRD PARTY'S design, not FSD's** — and this overturns the
first explanation offered here. Scott to Dave at FSD, 2024-06-17: *"I just
ordered your G1000 PCBs and then ordered **littlehelpers shield which I think
replaces a shield in your kit**."* Peter Eier's shield is a community
replacement for the one in FSD's own kit, which is why it carries a different
designer credit, a different panel number, and reads "Shield **for**
FlightSimMaker G1000 NXi". A FlightSimDIY→FlightSimMaker rebrand does appear to
have happened, but it is NOT why these boards look like two suppliers.

**Boards came from TWO sources, and this took two wrong turns to establish.**

- **FSD, 2024-06-12 · $43.67** — FSD G1000 v2 + G1000 Blank (4) PCB Set
- **FSD, 2024-06-18 · $55.61** — FSD GMA1347 + GMA-1347 Blank (2) + another
  G1000 Blank (4) PCB Set
- **JLCPCB `W202406142358683`** — reviewed 2024-06-14, shipped 2024-06-17.
  Contents: **`Gerber_G1000_nxi_v2_shield_rev...` — 10 pcs**

**The shield is the ONLY board Scott had fabbed.** Checked against his complete
JLCPCB history — ten orders since 2019, and the only other 2024 one was
`Gerber_PCB_FFB_Arduino_Yoke_1...`, unrelated. Everything else came from FSD as
physical PCBs; "Blank (4) PCB Set" means four boards, not four files.

**Shields reconcile exactly:** 10 fabbed = 7 unbuilt + 1 built + 2 sold.

**FSD boards do not:** 4 + 4 + 2 = **10 paid for, 5 on the bench.** Scott,
2026-08-29: *"maybe they're just yet to be discovered over on the wire
shelves."* Most likely — that is a search, not a purchase.

**Scott owns the design files, permanently, and can re-fab anything.** The FSD
store emails list a **Downloads** section and a **Tracking** section on the same
order — `FSD G1000 v2` and `FSD GMA1347`, both **"Expires: Never"** — alongside
physically shipped blank PCB sets. Both were bought, not one or the other. An
earlier claim here that the panels could not be re-fabbed was wrong.

**Two architectures, and they do not mix.**

- **NXi** — one shield carries *both* MEGAs. Its silkscreen says "includes
  GMA1347 Control Board", and build #2 proves it.
- **Original** — separate boards, each with its own MEGA.

**HOW IT ACTUALLY WENT** — Scott's reconstruction, 2026-08-29, and it fits every
piece of evidence:

1. Bought two FSD **G1000 4-PCB sets**: each is control board + left + right +
   softkeys.
2. Then found **Peter Eier's shield**, which is more complete — one board
   carrying *both* MEGAs, for the G1000 NXi and the GMA1347 together.
3. **Repurposed the FSD daughter boards** — left, right, softkeys — onto Peter's
   shield. They fit because FSD's control board and Peter's shield drive the
   *same* FSD panel set; he built a better host for boards that already existed.
4. Which leaves **both** FSD control boards stranded by a single decision.

That last point matters and was missed here earlier. The G1000 Control Board and
the GMA1347 Control Board are not two separate orphans with two separate
stories — **Peter's shield replaced both at once**, because it absorbs both
roles. One choice, two redundant boards.

**ONE COCKPIT, NOT TWO UNITS.** A G1000 suite is PFD + MFD with a single
GMA1347 between them. Scott: *"there's only one audio panel per build. It sits
between the two g one thousands."*

### Stop deriving quantities from receipts

Three counts were published in this file today and all three were wrong: "four
missing", then "five missing on the wire shelves", then "one missing". Every one
came from reading line items and inferring what must physically exist. Every one
was corrected by Scott looking at the actual boards.

The receipts are excellent for **provenance** — who sold it, when, for how much,
which files came with it. They are unreliable for **quantity**, because a line
item does not say how many boards are in a "set", what went into a build, or
what was sold on. **Count the pile. Measure, do not model.**

### Why the GMA1347 control board is redundant, and why that is not obvious

The GMA1347 **audio panel is still correct for NXi** — commercial NXi panel sets
still ship one, and FSD still sells the FSD GMA1347. What changed is *what
drives it*. In the original architecture the audio panel had its own controller
board and its own Arduino. In NXi, the shield absorbs that: `U4 MEGA 2560 PRO —
GMA1347`.

So the **panel** carries over and the **control board** does not. That
distinction is the whole answer, and it is easy to get backwards.

Build #2 settles it by demonstration rather than inference — the shield drives
the audio panel over two ribbons, with no separate control board present.

### The GMA1347 faceplate

The "GMA-1347 Blank (2) PCB Set" was the design for control board **plus**
faceplate. Only the control board has surfaced as a physical PCB. Either the
faceplate Gerber was never sent to JLCPCB, or it was and the boards are in the
same stack. **The faceplate is still wanted for an NXi build**, and if it was
never fabbed the files are already paid for — it is a JLCPCB order away, not a
repurchase.

**The folder inside is named `FSD G1000 v2.3` — matching the boards.** So the
purchased entitlement delivers the same revision that is on the shelf.

**THE 4-PCB SET IS NAMED IN THE GERBERS, so its composition is proven rather
than deduced:**

    Gerber_PCB_G1000 v2.3 Left side.zip
    Gerber_PCB_G1000 v2.3 Right Side.zip
    Gerber_PCB_G1000 v2.3 Ctrl Board.zip

Left + Right + Ctrl Board + Bottom. That is exactly the four boards in a
"G1000 Blank (4) PCB Set", and **every one of them can be re-fabbed at JLCPCB
from files already owned.** An earlier worry here that the panels were
FSD-only, because only the shield had ever been fabbed, is dead.

**KEPT LOCALLY BECAUSE THE EMAILED LINK CANNOT BE TRUSTED.** The companion
GMA1347 link from order #9581 returns HTTP 404 with WooCommerce's *"Invalid
download link."* — the TOKEN is rejected, not necessarily the entitlement. Note
the two keys are different generations: the G1000 one is 32-hex, the GMA1347 one
a UUID, so the store has regenerated its download permissions at some point and
the older token stopped matching.

**The entitlement is probably fine; the 2024 email is not.** A fresh link comes
from My Account → Downloads on flightsimdiy.com, which mints a current key.

That is exactly why the G1000 archive is now stored on the InvenTree server
rather than bookmarked. A link that says *Expires: Never* is a promise about
the entitlement, not about the URL in an old email — and one of the two had
already stopped working before anyone thought to test it.
