# G1000 sim panels — BO-0017

Provenance recovered 2026-08-29 from receipts and board silkscreens, because
none of it was written down anywhere. **Bullets first — the narrative below is

## The short version

**Two builds already exist.**

- **Build #1** — original G1000, **hand-wired**. Arduino UNO on a Keyestudio
  proto shield, several hundred point-to-point wires, orange 3D-printed
  bracketry. No PCB at all.
- **Build #2** — G1000 **NXi**, PCB-based. FlightSimMaker shield driving the
  GMA1347 audio panel over two ribbon cables. Working.

**One vendor, two brands.** FlightSimDIY became **FlightSimMaker**. Older boards
carry the FSD roundel, newer ones say FlightSimMaker. Both still trade — search
both names before assuming a board is discontinued.

**You bought DESIGNS from FSD and had the BOARDS made at JLCPCB.** Confirmed
2026-08-29, and it corrects an earlier wrong inference here that FSD shipped
physical PCBs.

- **FSD, 2024-06-12 · $43.67** — FSD G1000 v2 + G1000 Blank (4) PCB Set
- **FSD, 2024-06-18 · $55.61** — FSD GMA1347 + GMA-1347 Blank (2) + another
  G1000 Blank (4) PCB Set
- **JLCPCB order `W202406142358683`** — reviewed 2024-06-14, shipped 2024-06-17,
  FedEx `776884894482`. Contents: **`Gerber_G1000_nxi_v2_shield_rev...` — 10 pcs**

**So there should be about TEN of the NXi shield, not one.** JLCPCB's minimum is
five and the order was for ten. The same probably applies to the side panels and
softkeys. **Go and count the stack before ordering anything.**

The board silkscreen codes are JLCPCB fab markings, not vendor stock numbers —
`240614`, `240615`, `240207` are fab dates, and 240614/15 match that order to
the day.

**Two architectures, and they do not mix.**

- **NXi** — one shield carries *both* MEGAs. Its silkscreen says "includes
  GMA1347 Control Board", and build #2 proves it.
- **Original** — separate boards, each with its own MEGA.

**Therefore:**

- ✅ Keep — the four NXi boards (#1152 shield, #1153 left, #1154 right,
  #1155 softkeys). These are build #3.
- ❌ **The GMA1347 Control Board v2.2 (#1156) and G1000 Control Board v2.3
  (#1157) have no target.** They are the PCB retrofit for build #1, and Scott
  has said he is not going back to it.
- 🔍 **Still needed for a build:** 2× MEGA 2560 PRO (#74, zero stock) and
  2× 74HC4067 mux (#21/#436, both zero stock). The LM2596 is covered — 15 at
  A3-R7C2.

**Brackets:** build #1's mechanical solution, for an UNO and a proto shield.
Nothing carries over to an NXi build. Scrapping, **except** the bracket that
mounts to the wide monitor's base support — and that one should be stored
**with that monitor**, not in a bracket pile.

## Detail

### The rebrand

The project moved from FlightSimDIY to the FlightSimMaker group. The June 2024
receipts sit exactly on the seam: PayPal merchant **FlightSimDIY**, contact
address **Contact@flightsimmaker.com**, card descriptor **PAYPAL \*FSM STORE**.
Buying mid-rebrand is why one pile of boards looks like two suppliers.

Recorded on the supplier record (#33) so it is not re-derived.

### Why the GMA1347 control board is redundant, and why that is not obvious

The GMA1347 **audio panel is still correct for NXi** — commercial NXi panel sets
still ship one, and FSD still sells the FSD GMA1347. What changed is *what
drives it*. In the original architecture the audio panel had its own controller
board and its own Arduino. In NXi, the shield absorbs that: `U4 MEGA 2560 PRO —
GMA1347`.

So the **panel** carries over and the **control board** does not. That
distinction is the whole answer, and it is easy to get backwards.

Build #2 settles it by demonstration rather than inference — the shield drives
the audio panel over two ribbons, with no separate control board present.

### The GMA1347 faceplate

The "GMA-1347 Blank (2) PCB Set" was the design for control board **plus**
faceplate. Only the control board has surfaced as a physical PCB. Either the
faceplate Gerber was never sent to JLCPCB, or it was and the boards are in the
same stack. **The faceplate is still wanted for an NXi build**, and if it was
never fabbed the files are already paid for — it is a JLCPCB order away, not a
repurchase.

### What was deliberately not done

- **No purchase orders.** Backfilling historical POs is a designed change queued
  in `OPEN.md` — born COMPLETE, no stock rows, marked reconstructed, off the
  live reference sequence. Improvising half of it here would create exactly the
  ambiguous records that design exists to prevent.
- **No stock rows.** There is no container yet. A location in this system is a
  *place*; inventing one for a box that does not exist is a record that lies
  about the shop. The parts exist and are searchable; the boards are not yet
  placed.
